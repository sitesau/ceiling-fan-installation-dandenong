Separate a planned job from an electrical fault

A dead power point, recurring breaker trip, scorched outlet or burning smell should be reported before discussing a new fitting. Electrician Dandenong's published guidance says a power outage, burning smell or breaker that will not stay up needs a licensed electrician rather than a DIY fix. Give the symptom, when it began and what changed immediately beforehand.

If the entire street is dark, check whether nearby properties are also affected before assuming the problem is inside the home. If only one circuit or outlet has failed, describe what remains operational and whether the breaker has moved. This information helps distinguish a planned improvement from fault finding.

For smoke, flames or anyone injured by electric shock, keep clear of anything live and call 000. For other problems, avoid presenting the symptom as an ordinary installation detail.

Make the quote specific enough to compare

The published local guidance says the job and its cost should be agreed before anyone opens a wall or switchboard. Ask the quote to identify the fitting location, switching arrangement, access assumptions, wiring changes and removal of an old fitting where relevant. Also ask what could alter the price if an issue is found after work begins.

Fault finding is priced once the electrician has seen the problem, so a request involving tripping, a dead outlet or scorching may need assessment before installation. The guide gives national quote guidance of $350 to $650 for connecting an oven, cooktop or rangehood as at October 2026. That figure is not a ceiling fan price and should not replace a project-specific quote.

  • Confirm what materials and labour are included.
  • Ask whether access or existing wiring changes the scope.
  • Request written agreement on variations before extra work starts.

Confirm the responsible people and paperwork

In Victoria, licensed electricians carry out regulated electrical work, while the registered electrical contractor running the job carries the insurance, according to the published FAQ. One person can hold both roles, while a larger crew may have several electricians working under one contractor. Ask who will be on site and who is responsible for the finished work.

An apprentice may help, but a licensed electrician supervises regulated work and provides the Certificate of Electrical Safety when it is finished. Ask whether the proposed work requires that certificate and how it will be supplied. Keep the quote, agreed changes and certificate together for the property records.

The same guidance states that services must be provided with due care and skill under the Australian Consumer Law. That makes a clear written scope useful if the work changes during the visit.
